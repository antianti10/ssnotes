The Scientific Revolution

Module 18, Lesson 1 — Box & Bullet Notes

Scholars began questioning old beliefs and using experimentation to build new theories.
  • Renaissance sparked curiosity
  • Reformation challenged religion
  • New view of world
The Roots of Modern Science: scholars once trusted ancient authorities over observation.
  • Geocentric theory: earth-centered universe
  • Crusades, exploration exposed new ideas
  • Navigation fueled astronomy, math
A Revolutionary Model of the Universe: new evidence challenged the geocentric theory.
  • Copernicus: heliocentric theory (1543)
  • Kepler: planets orbit elliptically
  • Galileo: telescope; forced to recant
The Scientific Method: a logical process for testing ideas emerged.
  • Question, hypothesis, experiment, conclusion
  • Deductive vs. inductive reasoning
  • Bacon: experiments; Descartes: logic
Newton Explains the Law of Gravity: he unified earlier discoveries into one theory.
  • Law of universal gravitation
  • Published Principia Mathematica (1687)
  • Believed in deism
The Scientific Revolution Spreads: the scientific method reached new fields.
  • New tools: microscope, barometer, thermometer
  • Vesalius, Jenner advanced medicine
  • Boyle founded modern chemistry
